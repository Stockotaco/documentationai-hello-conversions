**Reporting → Revenue → Lead LTV** is organised by the day the *lead* arrived, not the day money arrived: a lead captured on 1 March is followed forward — revenue after 1 day, 7, 30, 90 — whatever month those payments landed in. With a three-week sales cycle, a report keyed on payment date always credits this month's revenue to this month's spend, and you never see that January's leads were twice as valuable as March's.

## What it needs connected

| Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **[GoHighLevel](/connect/sources/gohighlevel) contacts** | Leads *are* contacts. Without them there is no report. |
| **GoHighLevel transactions** | The revenue side. Revenue has to be tied to a contact. |
| **[Meta Ads](/connect/sources/meta-ads) / [Google Ads](/connect/sources/google-ads)** | Optional. Adds Spend, CAC, Payback and LROAS. |

<Callout kind="alert">
  **Stripe alone will not work.** Stripe charges arrive without a link to a contact, so there is nothing to attach revenue to. A project in that state shows a **"Stripe-only — Lead LTV unsupported"** chip in the page header instead of a page of zeros. Connect GoHighLevel to enable the report.
</Callout>

## What counts as a lead

A lead is **a contact with an email address**, dated by when the contact was created — hence the first KPI being *New Leads w/Email*. Contacts captured with only a phone number never enter this report, so a call-only funnel shows a lower lead count here than in GoHighLevel. The gap is exactly those contacts.

## What counts as revenue

GoHighLevel transactions, net of refunds, tied to the contact who produced them, dated relative to that contact's capture date:

- Statuses `succeeded`, `refunded` and `partially_refunded` are read and the refunded portion subtracted. Only a **positive net amount** contributes.
- Transactions **before** the contact's capture date are ignored, so a contact re-created in the CRM after buying looks like a zero-value lead.
- Amounts convert to **USD at fixed rates**, not the rate on the day of the sale. A currency the conversion table doesn't cover is dropped entirely rather than counted at face value.

<Callout kind="note">
  Fixed rates mean a multi-currency client's historic figures shift slightly against their own books. None of this applies to a single-currency USD account.
</Callout>

## The KPIs

| Tile | What it measures |
| --- | --- |
| **New Leads w/Email** | Contacts with an email captured in the window, after filters. |
| **Day 1 / 7 / 30 / 90 Value** | Total revenue those leads produced within that many days of capture. |
| **Lead → Customer Rate** | Share of those leads who have **ever** produced revenue — including after your window ended. |
| **Activation Rate** | Share who produced revenue within 30 days. The faster, comparable version of the above. |
| **Median Time to First $** | Median days from capture to first payment. Lower is better. |
| **CAC** | Ad spend in the window ÷ customers from it. |
| **Payback** | The first curve point where cumulative revenue per customer covers CAC. "Not yet" means it hasn't by Day 90. |
| **LROAS** | Day 90 Value ÷ ad spend. 1× is break-even by day 90; 3× is three dollars back per dollar in. |

Day values are **cumulative** — Day 7 includes everything Day 1 counted, so the curve only goes up.

<Callout kind="warning">
  **Lead → Customer Rate looks forward past the end of your window.** It asks whether a lead has ever converted, so the same March window reports a higher rate in June than in April. When comparing two periods use **Activation Rate**, which caps every cohort at 30 days.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning">
  **CAC and LROAS divide total ad spend by all customers, not just paid ones.** Spend is every dollar of Meta and Google spend in the window; customers is every converting lead, however they arrived. On an account with strong organic flow this flatters CAC considerably — use the Source / Medium table for per-channel economics.
</Callout>

The **Total / Avg / Lead / % Change** toggle restates every value three ways. Quote Avg / Lead when lead volume changed between periods: totals move with volume, averages don't.

## Attribution

| Mode | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Last Touch** (default) | The most recent source the contact arrived through. |
| **First Touch** | The source they originally arrived through. |

With no UTM, Hello Conversions falls back to the session source on the contact, then to `(direct)` / `(none)`. Values are lower-cased, and where the CRM stored several comma-joined values in one field only the first is used — hence `facebook` rather than `facebook, ig, direct`.

Switching modes redraws every breakdown but changes no lead count or revenue total.

## The breakdowns

**Lead LTV by Cohort.** Leads bucketed by capture date at day, week or month granularity.

<Callout kind="info">
  The cohort table deliberately reaches back **further than your date range** — 30 days, 12 weeks or 6 months beyond the start, depending on granularity — so a two-week-old cohort has mature cohorts to compare against. Rows outside your selected range are expected here, and only here.
</Callout>

**Lead LTV by First Form Submission.** Each lead filed under the **first form they ever submitted**. Leads that never submitted a form are grouped as *No form submission* rather than dropped.

**Lead LTV by Source / Medium.** Leads by resolved source and medium, with spend attached where it can be. Understand the spend column before presenting it:

- **Google** spend is filed by campaign type — Search, Shopping and Performance Max under `cpc`, Display under `display`, Video under `video`, Demand Gen and Discovery under `paid_social`.
- **Meta** spend is split across your Facebook and Instagram rows in proportion to the leads each claimed, but only when Meta-attributable leads are at least **5%** of the period's leads. Below that the split would be noise, so all Meta spend appears as a single `(meta-paid)` row with no leads against it.

<Callout kind="tip">
  A `(meta-paid)` row with all your Meta spend and zero leads means Meta traffic is arriving without usable attribution — almost always missing or broken UTMs on the ads. A tracking problem to fix, not a reporting quirk.
</Callout>

## Reading a maturity-limited number

A cohort captured eight days ago has a Day 30 cell containing revenue through day eight. The report marks these cells and shows the cohort's age on hover, but the trap is the comparison: the newest cohort looks worse on Day 30 and Day 90 purely because it hasn't had time. Compare like-aged cohorts, or read down Day 1 and Day 7.

## Drilling into a number

Clicking a **New Leads** figure — on a KPI or any breakdown row — opens the contacts behind it with source, medium, campaign, capture date, whether they converted and their lifetime value, carrying the page's active filters plus that row's dimension.

## Freshness

Lead LTV reads a snapshot rebuilt **once a day, at a set hour in the project's own timezone**. The header chip shows the last successful rebuild.

<Callout kind="note">
  The refresh button re-runs the queries against the current snapshot; it does not rebuild it. If the header shows **Refresh failed**, you're on the last good build until the next scheduled rebuild succeeds.
</Callout>

## When a number looks wrong

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Report is empty, header says Stripe-only | GoHighLevel isn't connected |
| Lead counts lower than the client's own CRM count | Contacts without an email address are not leads here. Compare against a GoHighLevel view filtered to contacts with an email |
| A known customer shows as a zero-value lead | Their payments predate their contact record; revenue only counts from the capture date forward |
| Ad spend missing but leads and revenue are fine | Meta Ads or Google Ads isn't connected, or hasn't synced. Spend, CAC, Payback and LROAS go blank together, which is the tell |
| Everything went to zero after a client shared a link | A shared URL carries its filters. If they match nothing in the current window, an amber banner names each filter that matched no leads, with a one-click clear |
| Numbers don't match [Marketing Efficiency](/reporting/marketing-efficiency) | Same lead data, different revenue counting: this report follows leads forward from capture, that one counts transactions inside the window. The two agreeing would be the surprise |

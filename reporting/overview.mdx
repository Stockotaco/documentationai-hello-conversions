title: Reporting

**Reporting** is the read-only half of a project. Everything here is derived from the data your sources send — no report has its own settings, and none of them can show you a number the underlying source never sent.

That single fact explains most "why is this empty?" questions, so it's worth stating plainly: **a report stays blank until the source it reads from is connected and has synced.** The rest of this page is the map of which report needs what.

## What's in the section

Open a project and pick **Reporting** from the icon rail. The section nav groups the reports:

| Group | Report | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue** | [Overview](/reporting/revenue-overview) | What did we collect, from how many customers, over this window? |
| | [Lead LTV](/reporting/lead-ltv) | What is a lead captured this month worth 1, 7, 30, 90 days later? |
| | [Marketing Efficiency](/reporting/marketing-efficiency) | What did we spend, what came back, and per channel and campaign? |
| **Meta Ads** | Creatives | Per-creative spend and performance from the Meta Ads source. |
| | Frameworks | The same creative data grouped by creative framework. |
| | Tracking | Whether your Meta ads carry the tracking they need. |
| **Email** | Email Stats | Sends, opens, clicks and health for GoHighLevel email. |
| **Workflows** | Overview | Delivered, failed and filtered conversion volume. |
| | Activity Log | One row per delivery attempt, for tracing a single event. |

**Reporting → Revenue → Overview** is the default — landing on Reporting without picking a report takes you there.

The Workflows pair is different in kind from the rest. Those two read from the conversion-delivery pipeline rather than from a warehouse, so they populate as soon as a [connection](/conversion-tracking/connections) starts producing runs. [Activity & Run History](/conversion-tracking/activity-history) covers them in detail.

## What each report needs connected

Everything under Revenue, Meta Ads and Email is built from the data your [sources](/connect/sources-vs-destinations) sync. Connect the source, wait for the first sync, then the report fills in.

| Report | Needs | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue → Overview** | A revenue source: [GoHighLevel](/connect/sources/gohighlevel) or [Stripe](/connect/sources/stripe) | No revenue, customer or transaction figures |
| | GoHighLevel, for the form and product panels | Forms, Landing Pages and Products stay empty |
| | The [tracking script](/help-center/guides/install-tracking-script) | Page views, unique visitors and conversion rate stay at zero |
| **Lead LTV** | GoHighLevel — both contacts and transactions | The report can't be built at all |
| | [Meta Ads](/connect/sources/meta-ads) / [Google Ads](/connect/sources/google-ads) | Leads and revenue still work; spend, CAC, payback and LROAS are blank |
| **Marketing Efficiency** | GoHighLevel — both contacts and transactions | No leads, no sales, no customer classification |
| | Meta Ads / Google Ads | Spend, MER, CAC, and the whole campaign table are empty |
| **Meta Ads** (all three) | Meta Ads | Nothing to show |
| **Email Stats** | GoHighLevel | Nothing to show |

<Callout kind="warning">
  **Stripe alone is not enough for Lead LTV or Marketing Efficiency.** Both reports work by tying revenue back to the contact who produced it, and Stripe charges arrive without a contact link. On a Stripe-only project Lead LTV shows an explicit "Stripe-only — Lead LTV unsupported" chip rather than silently reporting zeros. Revenue → Overview does work on Stripe alone — its revenue, customer and transaction figures are built directly from Stripe charges.
</Callout>

When a project has both GoHighLevel and Stripe, Revenue → Overview uses both: Stripe is treated as the truth for money it processed, matched to the GoHighLevel transaction that shares its payment intent, and GoHighLevel supplies anything Stripe never saw — cash, other processors, and the contact attribution.

## Date ranges

All three Revenue reports default to **the last 30 days** and are filtered by a date range you control at the top of the page. Revenue → Overview also offers 7d / 30d / 90d shortcuts.

Two things about that window are worth knowing before you argue with a client about a number:

- **Days are cut in the project's timezone**, not yours and not UTC. A sale at 11pm on the 31st in a project set to `America/New_York` belongs to the 31st, even if the person reading the report is in London. The timezone is a project setting, under **Settings → Project → General**.
- **The comparison period is the same length, immediately before.** Every trend arrow and "vs. previous" figure compares your window against a window of identical length ending the day before your start date. Pick 12 days and you're compared against the 12 days before that — not against last month, and not against the same period last year.

Revenue → Overview and Marketing Efficiency also have a day / week / month granularity toggle, which changes how the chart and the breakdown table bucket the same underlying data. It does not change the window.

## Filters

Revenue → Overview has no filters beyond the date range — it is the whole project.

Lead LTV and Marketing Efficiency both filter on attribution:

| Filter | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Attribution: Last Touch** (default) | Attribute each lead to their most recent source, medium and campaign. |
| **Attribution: First Touch** | Attribute each lead to the source they originally arrived through. |
| **Source / Medium** | Restrict to leads whose resolved source or medium matches. |
| **Form** | Restrict to leads whose first-ever form submission was this form. |
| **Campaign** | Restrict to leads attributed to this campaign (Marketing Efficiency only). |

Marketing Efficiency lets you pick several values per filter; Lead LTV takes one of each.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Lead LTV keeps its full filter state in the page URL and has a **Copy link** button. Paste that link into a client email or a Slack thread and the recipient opens exactly the view you were looking at — same dates, same filters, same attribution mode. If a link is old enough that its filters no longer match any lead in the window, the page says so in an amber banner rather than showing an unexplained empty report.
</Callout>

## Freshness

The reports don't all read from the same place, and it shows up in how current they are.

| Report | How current |
| --- | --- |
| **Revenue → Overview** | Queried on demand, with results cached for 15 minutes to an hour depending on the panel. |
| **Lead LTV** | Built from a snapshot rebuilt once a day, in the project's local timezone. |
| **Marketing Efficiency** | Spend and lead data from that same daily snapshot; sales figures queried live. |

Lead LTV and Marketing Efficiency each show a **Data as of …** chip in the page header with the exact time of the last successful rebuild, and a **Refresh failed** chip if the most recent attempt errored — in which case you're looking at the previous good snapshot, not at nothing.

<Callout kind="note">
  The refresh button on those two pages re-runs the report's queries against the current snapshot. It does not rebuild the snapshot, so a figure that's stale because yesterday's rebuild failed won't move until the next rebuild succeeds.
</Callout>

Behind all of it sits your source sync schedule. A report can only be as current as the last sync from GoHighLevel, Meta or Google — if ad spend looks a day behind, check the source before you suspect the report.

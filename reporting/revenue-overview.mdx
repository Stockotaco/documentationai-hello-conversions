**Reporting → Revenue → Overview** is the project's revenue summary. The page is headed *Revenue Operations* in the app; the nav calls it Overview because it's the first thing you look at.

It answers one question in several ways: **how much money came in over this window, from whom, and how did people reach us?** It has no attribution model, no channel split and no ad spend — that's [Marketing Efficiency](/reporting/marketing-efficiency). This report is the ledger view.

## Where the numbers come from

Every figure is derived from a daily roll-up of whatever revenue source the project has:

| Project has | Revenue is built from |
| --- | --- |
| **GoHighLevel only** | GoHighLevel transactions, net of refunds |
| **Stripe only** | Stripe charges, net of refunds, deduplicated |
| **Both** | Stripe for the charges Stripe processed, matched to the GoHighLevel transaction that shares its payment intent; GoHighLevel for everything Stripe never saw — cash, other processors |

Only live, settled money counts. GoHighLevel transactions are included at status `succeeded` or `refunded`; Stripe charges at `succeeded` and `livemode`. Test-mode transactions never appear.

The remaining panels come from elsewhere:

| Panel | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Form Contacts, Form Submissions, Top Forms, Form Submission Pages | GoHighLevel form submissions |
| Page views, unique visitors, conversion rate | The [tracking script](/help-center/guides/install-tracking-script) on your pages |
| Top Products | GoHighLevel transactions, grouped by product name |
| Customer List | The revenue source, one row per customer |

## The KPI tiles

<Callout kind="info">
  Every tile compares against a window of the same length ending the day before your start date, and shows the percentage change. Twelve days selected means twelve days compared.
</Callout>

**The two hero tiles:**

| Tile | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **New Customers** | People whose first-ever purchase fell inside this window. |
| **Total Revenue Collected** | All settled revenue in the window, net of refunds. |

**New vs. existing** is decided against a customer's **first purchase ever**, not their first purchase in your window. Someone who bought in 2023 and bought again today is an existing customer, and today's sale is existing revenue — no matter what date range you pick. That's what makes New Customers a genuine acquisition number rather than a restatement of activity.

**The rest of the tiles:**

| Tile | What it counts |
| --- | --- |
| **Form Contacts** | Distinct contacts who submitted a form in the window. |
| **New Customers — Single** | Same figure as the hero tile. |
| **New Customer Rev — Single** | Revenue from transactions that landed on the buyer's first-ever purchase day. |
| **Existing Customers — Single** | Distinct customers who bought in the window but had bought before. |
| **Existing Customer Rev — Single** | Revenue from everyone else's transactions. |
| **Total Transactions** | Count of settled transactions in the window. |
| **Avg Order Value** | See the warning below — it is not total revenue ÷ total transactions. |
| **Form Submissions** | Count of form submissions — one contact submitting three forms is three. |
| **Conversion Rate** | Transactions against unique visitors, from the tracking script. |

<Callout kind="warning">
  **Avg Order Value and Conversion Rate are averages of daily figures, not the ratio of the totals.** Each day gets its own AOV, and the tile shows the mean of those daily values, so every day counts equally regardless of how many orders it had. A quiet Sunday with one $20 order pulls the number as hard as a Monday with two hundred orders.

  If a client computes AOV by hand from Total Revenue ÷ Total Transactions and gets a different figure, this is why, and their arithmetic is the one that matches the definition they have in their head. The same applies to Conversion Rate. When a number has to survive a client conversation, take the ratio from the two totals yourself.
</Callout>

## The panels

**Revenue chart.** New and existing revenue as stacked bars, with form submissions as a line over the top. Follows the day / week / month toggle.

**Top Products.** GoHighLevel transactions grouped by product name, with distinct customers and net revenue. This is GoHighLevel-only: a project whose revenue comes from Stripe alone won't populate it, because Stripe charges don't carry a product name in a form we can group on.

**Top Forms.** Each form and the number of distinct contacts it captured in the window, as a ranked bar. Contacts, not submissions — one person filling the same form twice is one contact.

**Form Submission Pages.** The same form data regrouped by the page the submission came from, with Submissions, Contacts, Customers and Revenue per page. This is the panel for finding the landing page that actually produces buyers, rather than the one everybody assumes does. Read its Revenue column with the caveat at the bottom of this page.

**Customer List.** One row per customer — Lead Date, First Purchase, # Purchases and LTV — with a click-through to that person's journey: when they were captured, which forms they submitted, and every purchase since.

<Callout kind="warning">
  The Customer List uses your date range to decide **which customers are listed**, not what their figures say. A customer appears if they bought at least once in the window; their **# Purchases and LTV are lifetime totals**. So a client who narrows to last week and sees a customer with 14 purchases and $9,000 of LTV is not looking at a bug — that's the customer's whole history, filtered to people who were active last week. It's a customer-quality signal, deliberately independent of the window.
</Callout>

## Drilling into a number

Most tiles and table cells are clickable. Clicking one opens the rows behind it — the actual contacts or the actual transactions that make up the figure — which is usually faster than arguing about a total. If a client disputes New Customers, open the drill and read them the list.

## When a number looks wrong

**Everything is zero.** No revenue source is connected, or the first sync hasn't landed. Check **Settings → Integrations → Sources**.

**Revenue is there but forms and products are empty.** The project is on Stripe alone. Those panels are built from GoHighLevel data.

**Page views, unique visitors and conversion rate are zero.** The tracking script isn't installed, or isn't on the pages people actually land on. See [Install the Tracking Script](/help-center/guides/install-tracking-script).

**Page views look right for recent dates and vanish for older ones.** Pixel data behind this report only reaches back **90 days**. Revenue goes back as far as your source synced; page views do not. A 6-month range will show revenue throughout and page views only in the recent portion — which also drags the conversion rate.

**A past day's revenue changed since last time.** Refunds are netted against the day of the original transaction, not the day the refund was issued. A refund processed today lowers the day the sale happened. This is intentional — it keeps a day's revenue meaning "what we actually kept from that day's sales" — but it does mean historical days can move, and a screenshot taken last month can legitimately disagree with the live report.

**A sale is on the wrong day.** Days are cut in the **project's timezone**, set under **Settings → Project → General**. A late-evening sale can land on the next day if the timezone is wrong for the client.

**Revenue here doesn't match Marketing Efficiency.** They count different things and neither is wrong. This report includes every settled transaction in any currency and, when both sources are connected, prefers Stripe's figure for Stripe-processed charges. Marketing Efficiency counts **USD transactions from GoHighLevel with a contact attached** and nothing else. On a project with non-USD sales, or sales that never got linked to a contact, Marketing Efficiency will report less. [Marketing Efficiency](/reporting/marketing-efficiency) covers its own definitions.

**Revenue in Form Submission Pages doesn't add up to total revenue.** It isn't meant to. The revenue credited to a page counts every purchase those contacts have ever made — including purchases from before your window and after it — and a contact who submitted forms on three different pages is credited on all three. Repeat submissions compound it further. Use the column to rank pages against each other, not to total revenue. The Customers column has the same lifetime framing: it answers "did this page ever produce buyers?", not "did it produce buyers this week?"

**The same form name appears twice in Top Forms.** Rows are per form *and* per page. A form embedded on more than one page produces one row per page, under the same name — the figures are split between them, not duplicated.

**Reporting → Meta Ads → Frameworks** (titled **Decision Frameworks** in the app) applies five decision frameworks to the creative data behind the [Creatives](/reporting/creatives) report, plus a ranked list of actions. Creatives answers "which ads are working?"; Frameworks answers "so what should I change?"

It reads the verdict and score for every creative in the current filters, and daily per-creative metrics for the top ten creatives by spend. Same [Meta Ads source](/connect/sources/meta-ads) requirement, date presets, objective and format filters, and "today is never included" clamp.

## Action Items

Generated, not written: up to five cards, in this priority order.

| Card | Triggered by | What it tells you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scale** | Any creatives with a Scale verdict — names the top 3 by score | Raise budget 20–30%, or duplicate into new ad sets |
| **Pause** | Kill-verdict creatives, **excluding** any rated low data sufficiency | Pause them and quotes the spend that frees up |
| **Fatigue** | A creative whose CTR fell more than 15% from its first third to its last third, or whose latest frequency is above 2.5 | Start testing new hooks before cost-per rises |
| **Format gap** | A campaign that beats the average ROAS but is missing a format used elsewhere in the account | Repurpose the winning concept into the missing format |
| **Budget drain** | Above-average spend with ROAS below 70% of average (or cost-per above 130% of average), and not a Scale | Cap the daily budget or move the money |

Fatigue detection needs at least five days of data for a creative; anything newer is skipped. The Pause card ignores low-spend Kills — a creative that spent $12 and got nothing is not evidence. If nothing qualifies the block doesn't render, meaning no creative crossed a threshold, not that the page failed.

## Performance Quadrant

A scatter of every creative: **spend on the X axis**, cost-per, ROAS or CTR on the Y. The crosshairs are the averages of the current set; dots are coloured by verdict. The quadrant names flip depending on whether high or low is good on the Y axis, which is the part people misread:

| Y axis | Top left | Top right | Bottom left | Bottom right |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ROAS or CTR (higher is better) | Hidden Gems | Winners | Cut | Budget Drains |
| Cost-per (lower is better) | Cut | Budget Drains | Hidden Gems | Winners |

"Hidden Gems" is always the low-spend, good-performance corner. "Budget Drains" is always high-spend, bad-performance.

<Callout kind="note">
  In cost-per mode, creatives with zero conversions are dropped from the chart entirely — plotting them at zero would put your worst performers in the Winners corner. Switch the Y axis to CTR to see everything.
</Callout>

## Hook Rate vs Hold Rate

**Video creatives only.** X axis is hook rate — 3-second views ÷ impressions. Y toggles between **hold rate** (views reaching 50% of the video ÷ 3-second views) and **CTR**, falling back to CTR alone if no hold-rate data has arrived. Reference lines sit at the set's average hook rate and average Y value:

| Position | Hold rate mode | CTR mode | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Top right | Grabs & Retains | Grabs & Converts | The whole creative works |
| Top left | Retains Without Hook | Converts Without Hook | Good content, weak opening — recut the first 3 seconds |
| Bottom right | Grabs, Loses Interest | Grabs, Doesn't Convert | Strong hook, weak payoff — the body or offer is the problem |
| Bottom left | — | — | Neither end works |

Empty for accounts running no video, which is expected.

## Winners Matrix

A heatmap of **campaigns down the side, ad formats across the top**. Each cell is the average ROAS (or cost-per, toggleable) of the creatives in that campaign-and-format combination, colour-graded, defaulting to ROAS for the Purchases objective and cost-per for every other.

The blank cells are the point: an empty cell is a format that campaign has never run — the reason Action Items flags format gaps. In cost-per mode, zero-conversion creatives are left out of the average, as in the quadrant chart.

## Creative Fatigue

A multi-line chart of the **top ten creatives by spend**, with **days since launch** on the X axis rather than calendar dates, so creatives that started weeks apart compare on the same curve. Y toggles between CTR, cost-per and frequency. Every line is a 3-day rolling average — which is why individual days won't match the Creatives report, and why one quiet weekend doesn't read as decay.

<Callout kind="warning">
  "Launch" means the first day the creative spent money **inside the selected date range**, not the day it was created in Meta. A creative running for three months shows day 0 at the start of your 30-day window, hiding its early-life curve. Widen the range to cover its actual start before reading the slope.
</Callout>

The fatigue chart also ignores the format filter: filtering to Video changes the quadrant, matrix and BCG charts but leaves the fatigue lines as the top ten by spend across all formats.

## BCG Matrix

Portfolio view. **X axis is spend share** — the percentage of the account's total spend in the range that went to that creative. **Y axis is ROAS** for the Purchases objective, or raw conversion count for every other. Bubble size scales with total spend, colour is the verdict.

The dividing lines are **medians, not averages** — unlike the Performance Quadrant. Medians resist a single enormous spender dragging the split line across the chart.

| Quadrant | Position | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stars** | High spend share, high return | Working at scale. Protect from fatigue, don't touch targeting |
| **Question Marks** | Low spend share, high return | Proven small. Increase budget gradually and see if it holds |
| **Cash Cows** | High spend share, return at or below median | Carrying the account but not excelling — watch for decline |
| **Dogs** | Low spend share, low return | Cut candidates |

## What makes this page misleading

Everything inherits the relative scoring described in [Creatives](/reporting/creatives), and both consequences cause arguments:

- **Every threshold is relative to the current view** — average spend, average ROAS, the median lines, the verdict colours. An account where everything is mediocre still produces Winners and Stars. These charts tell you which of your creatives is best, not whether it is good.
- **Changing the date range or the format filter re-draws every line.** Nothing is anchored to a fixed benchmark, so a screenshot is only meaningful alongside its range.

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Hook chart is empty | No video creatives in the range |
| Fatigue chart is empty or very short | Fewer than a few days of per-creative spend, or all creatives launched near the end of the range |
| Quadrant chart lost most of its dots | Cost-per mode with lots of zero-conversion creatives |
| Every chart is empty | No Meta spend in the range, or the objective is set to a result the account never records |
| Action Items shows nothing | No creative crossed a threshold — a normal state for a small or brand-new account |
| Charts disagree with the Creatives grid | Fatigue lines are 3-day rolling averages; BCG splits on medians, the quadrant on averages |

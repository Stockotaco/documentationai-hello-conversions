---
title: Team & Roles
description: The exact difference between an owner and a member, how organization membership differs from project membership, and how invitations are sent, accepted and revoked.
---

Access is two decisions. **Are you in the organization, and as what?** — everyone is an **owner** or a **member**, and there are no other roles. **Which projects can you see?** — owners see all of them, members see only the projects they've been added to.

## The two membership layers

**Organization membership** carries your role and is what **Organization settings → Team** lists. **Project membership** is a separate row per project; being in the organization does not by itself get you into a project.

The exception: **organization owners have implicit access to every project**, including ones created after they joined. They have no project membership rows, can't be removed from an individual project, and appear in a project's team list as **Org Owner** with no Remove button.

<Callout kind="note">
  This is why a project's team list can show more people than you added — everyone with explicit project membership, plus every organization owner.
</Callout>

## Owner versus member

Both roles do the work — connecting sources, building connections, reading reports. The difference is administrative.

| Action | Owner | Member |
| --- | --- | --- |
| See projects | Every project in the organization | Only projects they've been added to |
| Configure sources, destinations, connections and pipelines | Yes | Yes, in their projects |
| Edit a project's name, description and timezone | Yes | Yes, in their projects |
| Create a new project | Yes | Yes |
| Delete a project | Yes | No |
| View the organization's member list | Yes | Yes |
| Invite someone to the organization | Yes — any role, any project scope | Yes — as a member only, and only to projects they're in |
| Invite someone to a single project | Yes | Yes, in their projects |
| Revoke a project invitation | Yes | Yes, in their projects |
| Revoke or regenerate an organization invitation | Yes | No |
| Change someone's role | Yes | No |
| Remove someone from the organization | Yes | Only themselves |
| Add or remove a project's members | Yes | No |
| Rename or delete the organization | Yes | No |
| View usage | All projects | Their projects only |
| View or change billing | Yes | No |

Every rule is enforced on the server. A member who navigates straight to a billing URL gets a permission error, not a page.

<Callout kind="alert">
  Members can invite people — their one administrative power, and a fenced one. A member can only issue **member**-role invitations, and only to projects they belong to themselves. They cannot grant owner status or organization-wide access, so they cannot escalate anyone, including themselves.
</Callout>

## Inviting people

| Path | Who can send | What it grants | Expires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Organization settings → Team → Invite Member** | Any organization member | The role and project scope you choose | 7 days |
| **Generate Invite Link**, in the same dialog | Any organization member | The same choices, to anyone holding the link | 30 days |
| **Settings → Project → Team → Invite**, inside a project | Any member of that project | Fixed: **member** role, that **one project** | 7 days |

The email and link forms share three fields:

- **Email address** — only that address can use the invitation. One pending invitation per email per organization; a second attempt is rejected until the first is accepted or revoked.
- **Role** — Member or Owner. Only owners see this control; members always invite as members.
- **Project access** — *all current and future projects*, or *specific projects*. Only owners can grant the first, and it auto-adds the person to every project created later. Members must pick specific projects, and only ones they can access themselves.

A link is reusable: multiple people can accept it and it stays open until it expires or you revoke it. Treat it as a credential. Owners can **Regenerate** one from the pending invitations list, which issues a new token and immediately breaks the old one.

The project-scoped invite needs only an email address, which makes it the fastest way to give a client access to their own account without touching organization settings. Organization owners get a shortcut in the same dialog — *add an existing org member directly* — which skips the email for someone already in the organization.

## Accepting an invitation

The link lands on `/invite/<token>` and shows the inviter, organization, project and role.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in or create an account">
    If they're not signed in, the page offers **Sign In** and **Create Account**, then returns them to the invitation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept">
    This creates their organization membership at the invited role and adds their project memberships in one go.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Land in the right place">
    Single-project invitations open that project. Broader ones open the organization's projects list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An invitation is **pending**, **accepted** or **expired**. Expiry is evaluated when the link is opened or accepted — a past-date invitation shows "Invitation Expired" and can't be revived, so send a fresh one.

Someone already in the organization can still accept a project-scoped invitation: they keep their role and gain the named project.

Pending invitations are listed under the member list on both team pages, each with **Revoke**, which kills the link immediately.

## Removing people

**From a project** — **Settings → Project → Team**, Remove. Organization owners only. They lose that project and keep everything else.

**From the organization** — **Organization settings → Team**, Remove. Organization owners only, except that anyone can remove themselves. This also removes every project membership they held in that organization, so off-boarding someone who worked across ten client projects is one action, not ten.

Two guardrails:

- **The last owner can't be removed or demoted.** Promote someone else first — this is what stops an organization ending up with nobody who can reach billing.
- **Organization owners can't be removed from individual projects.** Change their role to member first, then manage project membership.

---
title: Match Quality
description: How Hello Conversions scores the identifiers sent with each Meta conversion, what a low score means, and what to change on your forms and funnels to raise it.
---

A conversion is only useful to Meta if Meta can work out *who* it belongs to. It does that by comparing the identifiers you send — hashed email, hashed phone, click IDs, IP address — against its own records. Match quality is our measure of how much you're giving it to work with.

## What the score is

Every Meta conversion we deliver gets a **match quality score from 0 to 100**, computed at delivery from the identifiers actually present in the payload. It's a coverage score: which of Meta's match parameters you supplied, weighted by matching power.

| Identifier | Sent as | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | `em` (hashed) | 3 |
| Phone | `ph` (hashed) | 3 |
| Facebook click ID | `fbc` | 2 |
| Facebook browser ID | `fbp` | 2 |
| Contact ID | `external_id` | 2 |
| First name | `fn` (hashed) | 1 |
| Last name | `ln` (hashed) | 1 |
| City | `ct` (hashed) | 1 |
| State | `st` (hashed) | 1 |
| Postal code | `zp` (hashed) | 1 |
| Country | `country` (hashed) | 1 |
| IP address | `client_ip_address` | 1 |
| User agent | `client_user_agent` | 1 |
| Gender | `ge` (hashed) | 1 |
| Date of birth | `db` (hashed) | 1 |

The weights add up to 22. Your score is the weight you supplied out of 22, scaled to 100 — so an event carrying email, phone, `fbc`, IP and user agent scores 45. Google Ads deliveries are not scored; the column is blank for them.

<Callout kind="note">
  This is **our** number, not Meta's. Meta's Event Match Quality reflects whether events actually matched real people on their graph, which depends on data we can't see. Ours only says how much identity we handed over: high coverage is a precondition for a good EMQ, not a guarantee, and the two won't agree exactly.
</Callout>

## Where to see it

Both live on **Reporting → Workflows → Activity Log**:

- **Per event** — a coloured badge on each delivered Meta row. Hover for the exact parameters that event carried.
- **Average** — the "avg match" figure in the header, across scored Meta deliveries over the last 7 days. Seven days rather than 24 hours because purchases are sparse.

| Score | Reads as |
| --- | --- |
| 50 or above | Green — healthy |
| 30 to 49 | Amber — matching, but leaving reach on the table |
| Below 30 | Red — most of these events probably match nobody |

Filtering the log to one connection re-scopes the average to it, which is how you find the event type dragging the number down.

## Where the identifiers come from

You don't set these by hand — each is picked up during enrichment, from whichever source has it.

| Identifiers | Source and handling |
| --- | --- |
| Email, phone, name | The CRM contact on the event. Email is lowercased and trimmed; phone is reduced to digits, with a `1` added to bare 10-digit US numbers, before hashing. |
| City, state, postal code, country | The contact record when GHL captured an address. Country falls back to `US`, so it's effectively always present. |
| `fbc`, `fbp`, `fbclid`, IP, user agent | GHL's attribution data first, then PostHog fills gaps. GHL's IP and user agent are preferred deliberately: PostHog's last-touch IP is often Facebook's click redirector rather than the buyer's device, and sending that hurts matching. |
| `fbc` | Passed through as captured, or synthesized from a raw `fbclid` when GHL recorded the click ID but not the formatted cookie value. |
| `external_id` | The CRM contact ID, sent unhashed. |

<Callout kind="warning">
  Meta rejects an `fbc` whose click is older than 90 days, and an expired one drags down matching instead of helping. We drop `fbc` values older than 85 days rather than send them — so a conversion that closes three months after the click legitimately scores lower, and there's nothing to fix on your end.
</Callout>

## What a low score actually means

Not that the delivery failed — the event reached Meta and the Activity Log shows it as `delivered`. It means Meta had little to match on, so a larger share of those conversions will never be attributed to an ad, which surfaces as Meta under-reporting your results and optimizing on a thinner signal.

Two events can score identically and behave differently: email plus phone (27) usually matches better than city, state, zip, country, IP and user agent combined (also 27), because email and phone are what Meta keys on. Treat the score as a coverage check, not a ranking.

## How to raise it

Ordered by how much they move the number.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collect email and phone on every form">
    Together they're 6 of the 22 available weight, and the two identifiers Meta matches on most reliably. A form asking only for an email caps every conversion well below 50. Making phone required on your main lead form is usually the single largest improvement available.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Install the tracking script on every page of the funnel">
    `fbp` and `fbc` — worth 4 together — only exist if the visitor's browser was seen before they converted, which includes the pages before the form. See [Install the tracking script](/help-center/guides/install-tracking-script).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep ad click IDs on your landing page URLs">
    `fbc` comes from the `fbclid` Meta appends to the click. If a redirect, URL shortener or funnel step strips query parameters, it's gone. Check that a real click on a live ad still shows `fbclid` in the landing page's address bar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask for the fields you can justify asking for">
    First name, last name, city, state and postal code are 1 each — five points of coverage for fields many forms already have. If the contact record has them, they're sent automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the contacts, not the conversions">
    Everything above is read from the CRM contact at delivery time, so a GHL workflow that fills in missing contact fields raises the score of every future conversion for that contact.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="tip">
  Changes only affect conversions delivered from that point on. Events already sent keep their score, so give it a few days of new volume before judging a fix, and compare using the 7-day average rather than individual rows.
</Callout>

## What won't help

- **Re-sending old events.** The identifiers won't be better the second time, and you'll create duplicates Meta may or may not collapse.
- **Filling in fake values.** A placeholder phone number is a hash that matches nobody, and it costs you the honest signal that the field is missing.
- **Chasing 100.** Gender and date of birth are never sent, and a conversion with no ad click can't carry `fbc`. A consistent green average is the goal.
